About

Dana P. Goldman is University Professor of Public Policy, Pharmacy, and Economics and the Founding Director of the Schaeffer Institute for Public Policy & Government Service at the University of Southern California. He also founded the Schaeffer Center for Health Policy & Economics, establishing it as one of the world’s premier health economics research groups. He previously served as Dean of the USC Price School of Public Policy from 2020 to 2024.

Dr. Goldman is the author of more than 400 articles with more than 28,000 citations. He pioneered a “Netflix model” to improve access to treatment and the benefits of lower copayments for the chronically ill. He serves (or has served) as a health policy advisor to the Congressional Budget Office, California Institute of Regenerative Medicine, Covered California, Edwards Lifesciences, Fred Hutchinson Cancer Institute, and GRAIL. He has been awarded more than $50 million in research funding from NIH. His research is regularly featured in outlets like the New York Times, Wall Street Journal, Washington Post, STAT, The Economist, NBC Nightly News, PBS NewsHour, CNN, NPR, and other media.

Dr. Goldman is an elected member of the National Academy of Medicine, National Academy of Social Insurance, and National Academy of Public Administration — three of his field’s highest honors. He is a past recipient of the USC Associates Award, Alice Hersh Emerging Leader Award, MetLife Silver Scholar Award, NIHCM Research Award, and Robert Biller Award, among other distinctions. In 2016, USC appointed him a Distinguished Professor and in 2024 a University Professor “for his decades of excellence as a policy leader and champion of interdisciplinary collaboration.”

Dr. Goldman co-founded EntityRisk, an AI startup producing software to expedite and improve health economics outcomes research, and Precision Health Economics, a boutique life sciences consultancy. EntityRisk pairs health economics and policy expertise with PROVEN™, its machine-learning modeling platform, to help biopharmaceutical companies predict health outcomes, set value-based prices, and decide which evidence is worth generating. He served on the Board of Directors of the Price Philanthropies and previously held the Distinguished Chair in Health Economics at RAND, where he directed the health economics program. He served as a nonresident senior fellow at The Brookings Institution, and is a research associate with the National Bureau of Economic Research. He graduated B.A. summa cum laude from Cornell University and earned a Ph.D. in Economics from Stanford University.
